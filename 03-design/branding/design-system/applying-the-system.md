# Applying the system

How the same language carries across the organizer, the guidebook and the mobile site. Mockups of each are in the companion design canvas.

## Organizer

ESTABLISHED: fits a glove box; prototype by customizing an existing zippered organizer; includes a document sleeve and space for a tire-pressure gauge and small tools; flashlight under consideration. Final dimensions and materials are open.

NEW recommendations:

**Concept 1: Zip-around clamshell (recommended for the prototype).** Opens like a book.
- *Left panel:* clear-window document sleeve labeled **MY IMPORTANT STUFF** (registration, insurance card), plus a slip pocket that holds the guidebook.
- *Right panel:* elastic loops sized to real tools, each with a printed label: TIRE GAUGE, FLASHLIGHT, PEN. Add a mesh zip pocket for small extras.
- *Why:* everything is visible at once, which supports the "find it in 30 seconds" test goal.
- *Prototype path:* buy a zip-around document or tech organizer, sew or iron on elastic loops, add heat-transfer vinyl labels, and swap in an orange zipper pull (3D-printed or acrylic bear/paw).

**Concept 2: Folio + tool pouch.** A slim document folio and a separate small zip pouch for tools, clipped together. It's more flexible for small glove boxes, but it's two things to find.

**Concept 3: Tri-fold roll.** A roll-up with three panels. It's compact and charming, but slower to use under stress and harder to prototype well.

**Exterior:** `lavender` shell, `orange` zipper and pull, Kit Bear patch or emboss on the front, a small checker tab on the spine edge. **Interior:** `surface`-colored lining for visibility in a dark car, with ink labels in Roboto Condensed.

**Sizing (NEW, needs measuring):** measure the glove boxes of 5–10 cars that your test participants drive before choosing a base organizer. A pen-style tire gauge is about 5–6 in. long; folded registration and insurance documents fit a sleeve of roughly 4.5 × 9.5 in. Size the shell to the smallest glove box you find.

## Guidebook

NEW recommendations:

- **Size:** quarter-letter (4.25 × 5.5 in), so it fits the organizer's slip pocket and can be printed and saddle-stitched at home or a campus print shop.
- **Structure:** cover → How to use this kit → My important stuff → Tire pressure → Oil level → Dashboard lights → What should I do? → QR back cover. These match the site's navigation, so moving between them is seamless.
- **Spread pattern:** left page = big headline + "why it matters" in 2 sentences; right page = numbered steps (orange disc numerals) with one diagram each.
- **Covers and section openers** are loud (lavender + checker + bear). **Step pages** are calm (`surface`, ink, one orange highlight per diagram).
- **QR code** on the back cover and inside the organizer flap, with a short URL printed underneath, since QR scanning sometimes fails.

## Mobile site

ESTABLISHED: mobile-first; large topic cards; short steps; clear next steps; working nav labels My Important Stuff, Basic Car Checks, Dashboard Lights, What Should I Do?

NEW recommendations:

- **Home:** a short greeting ("Life happens. Let's figure it out."), then four big topic cards (`radius-lg`, each with an icon) and one pinned card: **Something's wrong right now** → What Should I Do?
- **Task pages:** one step per card, with progress shown ("Step 2 of 5"). Big Back and Next buttons. "Done" ends on a proud-bear completion screen with the checker accent.
- **Dashboard Lights:** browse by status group (Stop soon / Check soon / FYI) with symbol + label grids. If the AI photo feature is built, it sits *beside* manual browsing as "Snap a photo of the light," never in place of it, and its answer always shows the matching symbol for the user to confirm.
- **Offline-friendly:** keep pages light so they load on weak signal; the guidebook covers no-signal cases.
- **Night use (to consider):** a dark theme (ink background, surface text) is worth testing for roadside use at night.
