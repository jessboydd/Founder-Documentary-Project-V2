# Illustration and imagery

ESTABLISHED: friendly, simple illustrations; strong photography areas; no complicated drawings.

NEW rules:

## Illustration

- **Flat shapes, brand palette only.** Lavender fields, ink details, one orange highlight that points to *the thing to look at* (the valve stem, the dipstick handle, the light on the dash).
- **Orange = "look here."** In every instructional diagram, orange marks the part the user needs to touch or find. This turns the accent color into a teaching tool.
- **Simplify car parts honestly.** Remove clutter, but keep enough real shape that the user can recognize the part in their own car. Under-hood diagrams show the *general* location and add "Your car may look different. Check your manual."
- **One idea per drawing.** A step diagram shows one action. Use arrows in `ink`, 3px with rounded ends.
- **Hands, not faces.** Show diverse hands doing the task. That keeps the focus on the action and lets any reader see themselves in it.

## Photography

- Real women in real cars: parking lots, driveways, everyday clothes. Bright, natural light.
- Close crops of hands on the gauge, the dipstick or the glove box.
- Color-block frames: place photos on lavender fields or with a checker corner to stay on-brand.
- Avoid stock "damsel by the roadside" imagery and any image that makes the reader look helpless.

## Checker accent

- Used as a finish-line motif at starts and ends, never under text.
- Tile size: 8px on screen, 3mm in print.
- Colors: `ink` + `surface`, or `lavender` + `surface`.

## Dashboard symbols

- Redraw simplified, single-weight versions inside the status shape (octagon, triangle or circle) with the status label under it.
- These are reminders, not replacements. Every symbol page ends with "Symbols vary by car. Your owner's manual has the exact meaning."
